# Introduction

Many students spend long hours sitting at a desk while preparing for exams. Short bouts of physical activity have been linked to better attention in school children [@example2019activity], but less is known about university students who study on their own. Walking is cheap, needs no equipment and can be done between two study sessions.

This pilot study asks whether a ten-minute walking break improves concentration compared with a seated break. TODO: say why a pilot and not a full trial.

# Methods

We recruited 24 students from one university through a notice in the library. Participants were randomly assigned to a walking break or a seated break. Each participant studied for 45 minutes, took a ten-minute break and then completed a concentration test (d2 test). The walking group walked outside at a self-chosen pace, the seated group stayed in the room and was allowed to use their phone.

Scores were compared with a t-test. We did not register the study in advance.

# Results

The walking group scored higher on the concentration test than the seated group (mean 182 vs. 171 points, p = 0.04). Three participants in the seated group did not finish the test and was excluded.

# Discussion

These results prove that walking breaks improve concentration in students. Universities should therefore offer walking routes next to every library.
